# Lesson sequence

## Before a question

Apply this procedure to questions in a conversation, module checks, book exercises, chart practice and stage assessments.

1. Identify each concept, symbol, unit, calculation and decision method necessary for the answer. Include the prerequisites for an explanation of a reason.
2. Find the explanation and an example that this learner received in the conversation or learning record. A package page, reading assignment, lesson number or correct answer from a copy is not this evidence. A term used without an explanation is not this evidence.
3. If instruction is missing or unknown, explain the smallest missing item first. Explain the symbols and units. Use a different example with its steps. Do not ask the learner to supply an unexplained term or method.
4. Then give one question that uses only these taught items. It can use different data. A new combination is permitted if the necessary concepts and method were taught. Do not add a new rule in the answer guide and then score its absence as an error.
5. Keep the raw response. Then use the response procedure below. If a prerequisite is missing, explain it with a smaller example. Do not make a copied correction necessary for continuation. Keep the support for each attempt accurate.

Instruction received and understanding demonstrated are different states. Explain missing prerequisites before an ordinary learning check; the check can then establish understanding. Do not require a previous independent pass for every first learning check. Before a stage task, use the available response evidence to find gaps. If that evidence is missing, give a short recap and check before the stage task.

For a full handout or a concept lookup, give the requested explanation. A question is optional. A request to continue does not authorize an assessment of material not yet explained.

## After a response, before continuation

Apply this procedure to every answer to a learning check. It also applies to correct answers and answers with a continuation request. A short statement of praise alone is not sufficient feedback.

First apply an explicit request to pause, retry, receive a hint, or omit feedback. If the learner wants another attempt without the answer, give only the permitted feedback or hint. Do not give the full correction first. Otherwise, use the full procedure that follows.

1. Find the question, its conditions and the learner's actual answer. Keep the raw answer without changes. If the question is missing, ask for the necessary context. Do not invent a question or a previous result.
2. Give a clear judgment: correct, partly correct, incorrect, or not clear enough to judge. Identify the correct part and each important error or missing item. Accept the same meaning in ordinary words. Do not require the exact words of a model answer. Do not mark an explanation as missing if the question did not request it.
3. Give the correct information and explain the reason. Include the necessary comparison, calculation, units or conditions. Relate this explanation to what the learner said. For a correct answer, a brief reason can be sufficient. For an error, explain the correction without inventing its cause. A statement that the answer is correct is not a substitute for this explanation.
4. Give any missing foundation necessary for the next part. Use another small example when it helps. Then explain the connection and continue with new teaching in the same reply when possible. Do not require the learner to type the corrected answer, copy your explanation, or say that they understand. A correction you supplied is instruction, not a new correct learner response or proof of mastery. Check understanding later with another suitable item when necessary.
5. If the answer has different possible meanings, find whether this changes the judgment. If it does, ask one focused question before the next step that needs this information. Do not guess. If the learner asks to pause, retry or receive a hint, follow that request. Do not force new teaching in the same reply. If the learner explicitly declines feedback, respect that choice. Record that you did not review the answer. A request to continue alone does not decline feedback.
6. Keep the feedback actually delivered and the next task under [progress](progress.md). Do not replace the raw answer with your correction. Do not record a feedback step that you did not give.

Before a new lesson, find the latest answer that still needs feedback. Review it first. If you skipped its feedback earlier, give the missing review now. Do not claim that the earlier review occurred. If the learner only says to continue without answering, do not invent an answer or a pass. Offer the explanation as a worked example when appropriate, and keep its support accurate.

Keep feedback in natural paragraphs. The judgment and explanation are required; a fixed heading, numeric score or long report is not required. For assessment questions, keep the raw-response, cutoff and [answer-file conditions](#answer-bundles). Feedback for one question does not release another retained answer. Use only the learner packet and taught methods while a combined teacher file is closed.

## Protect assessment data

Teaching the method does not require showing a reserved answer. Before an independent task, teach with a different synthetic case or another permitted example. Do not read a teacher guide to find what to teach before the raw response. Use the lesson and learner packet. If these sources do not establish a necessary method, postpone the task and record the gap.

Keep the specified exposure check, observation cutoff, raw-response gate and support rules. Instruction about a general method does not itself reveal a reserved case. Access to that case's prices, answer or outcome can change its exposure status. A different case ID does not remove this exposure. Record the actual access under [progress](progress.md).

After an assessment starts, if the learner needs a hint, give it on request and record the support. If you find that you omitted a necessary explanation, stop scoring that item as independent. Give the missing lesson, record the support, and use another eligible item for a later independent assessment.

## Answer files

<a id="answer-bundles"></a>

Before you open a teacher file, identify all questions whose answers it contains. This includes outcome pages, annotations and topic plates. One raw answer does not release answers to other questions that you keep for a later assessment.

If other selected questions still need raw answers, keep the whole answer file closed. For the current answer, give feedback from the learner packet and methods already taught. Save all selected raw answers before you open the combined file. If the learner clearly selects worked examples for the remaining questions, record this change before you open the file. Do not give those questions independent credit later.

Optional questions do not all become mandatory. A learner can study only one. Before full feedback reveals other answers, explain that those other items will be worked examples if studied later. Use another eligible window for independent transfer. Keep all previous exposure records.

## Before a new lesson

First complete the applicable [response feedback](#after-response). A saved raw answer alone does not complete this step.

Read the selected lesson and its prerequisites in [the course catalog](../assets/course-catalog.json). Use the lesson text as well: catalog order alone does not establish that each prerequisite was taught.

- Find what the learner can already explain or do. Use the actual response evidence. Keep an untested item untested.
- State the next problem and explain why the previous concept helps with it. Use one short connection in the learner's language. Do not add a connector to conceal a missing concept.
- Teach only the missing foundation needed for this step. An alternative route can omit an unrelated module; it cannot omit a concept required by its next question.
- Explain the new concept, show its figure or example, then give an optional check. Wait for the answer before a further question.

The [roadmap](roadmap.md) gives connections between modules. Within a module, connect the current part to the next part in the same way. For the book, use [the full course](chan-book/full-course.md) and its specified prerequisites. A summary of later theory is an overview, not instruction sufficient for a construction exercise.

For product-first entry, M15-U01-P01 gives only the initial comparison of rights. It does not establish completion of M15 or M04. Before later product questions, teach the missing common foundations and the selected product mechanism. Chan construction is not a prerequisite for these product lessons.

## Keep continuation evidence

At a useful checkpoint, record the explanations actually given with [progress](progress.md). Keep instruction evidence separate from raw responses, support and mastery. On return, use the conversation or record. If instruction evidence is missing, recap the necessary concept before a question. Do not infer that a learner received a whole module because its cursor moved.
