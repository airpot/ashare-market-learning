# Stage 01 tutor guide

Before you read this guide, keep the raw response. Accept equivalent explanations with full indications for the results. Also accept statements that the result is unknown, with full indications for these statements. These are exercise tasks. They are not fee or lot-size statements applicable at this time.

- A1: The fill is 100 shares. 200 stay pending. Submission without a fill does not make a holding.
- A2: Confirmation of cancellation or subsequent exchange or broker status is necessary. A fill can occur during the interval between the cancel request and its confirmation. Compare the last filled and canceled numbers of shares with the reports.
- A3: a secondary trade usually gives consideration from one investor to a different investor. But an issuer financing transaction gets money for the issuer with its terms. Do not give the same cash flows for all share-related transactions as a known result.
- B4: Buy outflow is 3004. Sell inflow is 3114. Profit is 110. Return is 110/3004, approximately 3.662 percent. The raw price change is 4 percent. It is a different measure.
- C5: Price loss for each share is 0.8. The loss constraint gives floor((180-20)/0.8/100)*100 = 200 shares. The cash constraint is sufficient for 200 shares. Necessary cash is 4020 and loss from the plan is 180. Gaps, nonfills, fees from execution and other positions can change the loss from execution. This is not a maximum with a guarantee.
- D6: There is no mandatory selection of one stock. Do a check of the event for A that is not known. Do a check of liquidity for B. For C, wait for the given trigger. A watchlist is not an order. Keep missing information and the no-trade selection.

Important misconceptions include pending shares as shares in the learner holding and price return as net trade return. Other misconceptions include costs subtracted two times and loss from a plan as loss with a guarantee. Give a different explanation of how the specified statements are different. Then, give a new example with numeric values. Indications for an answer do not show that the learner can send an order for execution in a market.
